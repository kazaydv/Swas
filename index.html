Sleep Apnea

The airway can narrow enough to briefly block airflow entirely, night after night.

~1 Billion adults worldwide are estimated to live with obstructive sleep apnea - most of them undiagnosed. (Benjafield et al., Lancet Respiratory Medicine, 2019)

Left unmanaged long-term, it's a recognized risk factor for high blood pressure, heart disease, and type 2 diabetes.

SWAS isn't a treatment for sleep apnea that needs a doctor and, often, a sleep study. But building the nightly habit of nasal breathing is one small way to work with your body instead of against it. If you notice gasping, choking, or long pauses in breathing at night, that's worth a real conversation with a doctor.
